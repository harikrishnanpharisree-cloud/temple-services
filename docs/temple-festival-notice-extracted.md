Key invocation verses:

Translated description: The Devi is the Mother of the Universe (Jagat Matha) — the Parashakti from whom all creation springs, and the deity of creation, sustenance, and destruction. Though she assumed a fierce (Rudra) form to destroy the asura Darika, in her peaceful form she is Lalithambika, granting all wishes to sincere devotees while bringing ruin upon those who defy her. She is described iconographically as dark as a monsoon cloud, three-eyed, seated upon Vetalam (her demonic vahana), bearing sword, shield, a severed head, and Darika's head in her hands, garlanded with skulls, surrounded by Bhootas, Pretas, Pisachas, and Mathrukas as her retinue — the destroyer of terrible calamities. - **Sandhya Deeparadhana** — performed at dusk; grants all-round prosperity.
- **Athazhapooja Deeparadhana** — performed at the night pooja; its darshan is said to grant marital happiness.

## 5. Major Rituals — Legends & Meaning

These narrative explanations are strong candidates for an "About the Festivals" or "Rituals Explained" page.

### Kalamezhuthu Pattu (ritual floor-drawing with song) — held all 7 evenings at 7:30 PM
Devotees draw the divine form of Sree Bhagavathi on the temple-courtyard mandapam and sing of her deeds (apadanas), beginning after Athazhapooja each night. **Legend:** After slaying Darika, Devi's wrath would not subside; Ganapathi and Subrahmanya appeared before her disguised as orphaned children to console her, and her anger briefly cooled. But upon seeing her own image drawn on the rock that is the seat of her vahana Nandikeshan, her fury returned, and she declared: "Whoever draws my form and prays shall receive my grace and prosperity." Separately, when Narada Maharishi showed Lord Shiva the Devi's fearsome form (readied for Darika-vadham, with 64 arms and 64 weapons), Shiva blessed it, saying those who draw and worship the Kali form, and those who sing her praises, would be cured of disease and granted all prosperity. White, yellow, red, and black are the traditional colours used in the kalam (drawing). Once the song concludes, the drawing is ceremonially erased with flowers, and the kalam-powder is distributed as prasadam.
**Vazhipadu rate:** 1 day — ₹200; 7 days — ₹1,000.

### Uthram Pongala — main festival Pongala, March 4
A communal rice-offering ritual: devotees observing 7 days of vratham (fasting/austerity) light fires under hundreds of pongala pots simultaneously; as the fire rises, sins and sorrows are said to dissolve into the atmosphere. Symbolism given in the text: rice = the universe, the husk/ego = "Aham" (I-ness) that dissolves as rice cooks, jaggery = bliss, coconut = the body. Considered the highest of the five great sacrifices (Pancha Mahayajnas) — an "Anna Pooja" (food-worship) that is Amma Daivam (Mother Goddess) worship.
**Pongala materials:** available at the Devaswom counter for ₹500; Pongala offering fee ₹50 (₹100 if materials are brought from outside).

### Darikan Thookkam — 7th day, 8:00 PM
Depicts the arrogant asura Darika (made overconfident by Brahma's boon that only a woman not born of a womb could kill him) challenging every direction to battle. Enhanced with Chenda melam, Thellikkathikkal, and Thalappoli. The performer dresses in costume, makeup, and crown at home, does a home pradakshinam, then walks to the temple with the percussion troupe.

### Garudan Thookkam — 7th day, 8:45 PM
Devotees portray Garuda on traditional hanging/swinging apparatus decorated with lamps. **Legend:** For Darika-nigraham, Kalidevi (the Devi) went to war per Shiva's instruction. Darika's wife Danavathi, having pleased Parvati through austerity, was given smallpox (vasoori) seeds, which she hurled at the Devi during battle, afflicting her with pain. Her vahana Vethalam drank all of Darika's blood but remained unsatiated; the Devi then promised the Vethalam the blood of whoever next entered the battlefield. Unable to locate Kalidevi (who had gone off to battle), Shiva sent for Vishnu's vahana Garuda to search for her — but the Devi could not permit Garuda's blood to be taken by her word to the Vethalam. Understanding this through his wisdom, Garuda voluntarily offered his own blood; a single drop satisfied the Vethalam. This is the legendary origin of striking the gong (Chunda) at the end of Garudan Thookkam to mark the symbolic "spilling" of blood.

### Mudiyettu — 7th day, 11:00 PM onward
An extremely rare vazhipadu performed only at Bhagavathi shrines (Kavu). "Mudiyettu" means "taking on" Darika's severed head. **Legend:** When the 14 worlds suffered under the asura emperors Darika and Danavendra (made arrogant by Brahma's boon that no man could kill them), they sought refuge with Shiva; Narada informed Shiva of their misdeeds; Shiva created Sree Bhadrakali from his third eye, who wages fierce war and destroys them, concluding with Mudiyettu. Performed as a vazhipadu against smallpox, skin ailments, ill omens, and for wish-fulfillment and removal of adversity.

### Vadakkupurathu Guruthi (Great Guruthi ritual) — pre-dawn, close of the festival
A square platform is erected outside the northern Gopuram using banana stems, areca-palm flowers, banana clusters, and jaggery; kaithiri and stick-torches are lit. After a procession with drums (kottu, kurava), the Devi is seated on the peedom (pedestal) and Melshanti Brahmasri Appukuttan Namboothiri leads the Maha Guruthi. Rice powder, flower powder, and turmeric powder are mixed to make one vessel of "Ninam" and six vessels of "Guruthi" (a traditional turmeric-lime substitute for blood), which are poured on the platform. **Meaning:** After Darika-vadham, the blood-thirsty Devi is offered this blood-like Guruthi to appease her; history notes that in earlier times, actual animal-blood offerings were made. Concluded with Guruthi prasada distribution and the Devi's return to the Sreelakam (inner sanctum) for 7 days of closed nada, before the 7th-day (Ezhampooja) darshan.
**Vazhipadu rates:** Ninam — ₹200; Guruthi — ₹100; Sampoorna (Complete) Guruthi — ₹1,000.

### Maha Prasada Oottu (annadanam / feast) — all Punartham & Utsavam days
A large-scale food-charity ritual held at the sanctum of Bhagavathi and Sri Ayyappa Swami (lord of annadanam). Framed in the text as spiritually superior even to grand material donations (elephants, cows, gem-studded gold, land) because "Anna" (food) equals energy/life-force, and both the giver and receiver of food gain from it.
**Rates:** Utsava Prasada Oottu — ₹17,000; Punartham Prasada Oottu — ₹15,000; 2026 Utsava contribution — ₹700.

### Ezhampooja (7th/closing Pooja) — March 15, 2026 (Meenam 1, Thiruvonam) Sunday
The day the Devi, who has been in "peaceful slumber" for 7 days after the Guruthi, grants darshan again to devotees — described as extra-auspicious in 2026 for coinciding with both Ekadasi and Meena Sankramam. Includes a special 10:00 AM rite: **Akamkalathil Ezhaam Guruthi Tharppanam** (7th Guruthi offering within the inner enclosure). 12:30 PM: Ezhampooja Prasada Oottu — sponsored by Sreepathmanabhan M.K., Madathil, Kattikkunnu (2026).

## 6. Other Annual Observances

### Vishu Kani — April 15, 2026 (Medam 1), Wednesday
On Vishu morning, Sree Krishna Bhagavan's Vishukani (auspicious first-sight arrangement) is set up in the golden mandapam at the Devi's sanctum. Devotees may offer Vishu Kanikka.

### Pathamudayam (10th Sunrise) — April 23, 2026 (Medam 10, Punartham), Thursday
Considered the day the Sun's influence (strongest of the nine planets) peaks, making it highly auspicious for any undertaking. Devotees may observe a full 10-day vratham from Vishu day through Pathamudayam, or just observe Pathamudayam alone — emphasizing brahmacharya and avoidance of tamasic food, concluding with temple darshan and theertham.

### Vinayaka Chaturthi — September 14, 2026, Monday
6:30 AM Mahaganapathi Homam plus special poojas/vazhipadu for Sree Ganapathi Bhagavan, the sub-deity at the temple. Invocation verse: *"Adipoojyam Ganadhyaksham, Gauriputram Vinayakam, Mangalam Paramam Roopam, Sreeganesham Namamyaham!"*
**Rates:** Ashtadravya Ganapathi Homam — ₹301; Ganapathi Homam — ₹51 + 1 coconut.

### Ayilyam Pooja — November 2, 2026 (Thulam 16, Mannarasala Ayilyam day), Monday
Special pooja for the temple's serpent sub-deities, Nagarajavu and Nagayakshi. Main vazhipadus: Sarpapooja, Noorupalum, Thalichukodala, Ayilyampooja.

### Navaratri — October 18–21, 2026 (Kanni 1–4)
Celebrates the Devi's nine forms (Kumari, Trimurthy, Kalyani, Rohini, Kalika, Chandika, Saraswathi, Shambhavi, Durga) alongside the temple's sub-deities. Schedule: Oct 18 (Sun) Poojavaypu (book & weapon worship); Oct 19 (Mon) Durgashtami; Oct 20 (Tue) Mahanavami; Oct 21 (Wed) Vijayadashami with Poojayeduppu — 7:00 AM Saraswathi Pooja, 8:30 AM Pooja eduppu, 8:50 AM Vidyarambham (children's Harisree/first-letters ceremony), performed by Brahmasri Kadambanattillam Appukuttan Namboothiri.

### Mandalapooja — November 17, 2026 (Vrischikam 1) to December 27, 2026 (41 days)
41-day vratham period honouring Sree Dharmasastha (Ayyappan), the sub-deity enshrined at the temple; nada opens for pooja/vazhipadu, with Punartham Oottu held throughout.
**Main pooja:** Mandala Chirappu Pooja — ₹200.

### Deepavali — November 8, 2026, Sunday
Commemorates Sree Krishna's slaying of Narakasura and Sri Rama's return after 14 years in exile. Pooja, vazhipadu, and decorative lights are held at the temple.

### Dhanumasa Thiruvathira — December 24, 2026, Thursday
Shiva's birthday and the wedding anniversary of Shiva and Parvati; an important day for women, some observing vratham from Makayiram star. Gopis are said to have worshipped Karthyayani on this day to obtain Balagopalan (Krishna) as husband; new couples observe "Poothiruvathira." Pooja and vazhipadu held at the temple.

## 7. Sub-Deities (Upadevas) at the Temple

| Deity | Description (translated) |
|---|---|
| **Durga Devi** | One of five goddesses (with Saraswathi, Savithri, Radha, Lakshmi) who emerged to enable creation; grants intellect, sleep, nourishment, fame, liberation, and happiness; described as supreme and truthful. |
| **Yakshiyamma** | The consciousness-form of Parashakti as protector of karma; a Desa Devata (regional deity) believed to fulfil all requests of those who seek refuge; astrologically significant — often the first deity to manifest in a Prashnam (astrological consultation) done regarding the temple. |
| **Brahmarakshas** | The protective Brahma-tejas (divine energy), worshipped in the concept of a consciousness-endowed Vishnu; considered the owner of the temple land and holder of the Guru position. |
| **Nagarajavu (Vasuki) & Nagayakshi** | Serpent deities residing at the temple; worshipped as family/clan deities for progeny, wealth and prosperity — angering them is said to bring misfortune. Best offerings: Thalichu Kodala, Paal Payasam (milk payasam). |
| **Sri Ganapathi** | Removes obstacles; worshipped before undertakings (as the Devas did before Palazhi Madhanam). |
| **Sri Dharmasastha (Ayyappan)** | Fierce, luminous form who metes punishment/blessing in Kali Yuga; honoured especially during the 41-day Mandalapooja. |

## 8. Darshan Protocol (Temple Visiting Order)

Translated from the booklet's "ദർശനക്രമം" section:

1. Outside the Chuttambalam (outer temple courtyard), first bow and pray to Yakshiyamma, Brahmarakshas, Nagarajavu, and Nagayakshi.
2. Enter through the **eastern entrance**, praying to the Devi as you enter the Chuttambalam.
3. Turn **left** and walk to pray to Sri Ganapathi, Dharmasastha, and Durga Devi; bow, then proceed to the front of the Sreekovil to pray.
4. Turn left again, receive theertham (holy water) and prasadam from the Melshanti, and complete the pradakshinam (circumambulation).
5. Exit through the **northern entrance**.
6. **Entry through the southern and western entrances is prohibited.**
7. Devotees are asked to observe mounam (silence) and hold the memory of the darshan.
