### Full sense of the invitation letter

Addressed to "Dhanyatman" (blessed soul), the letter announces the **Thiruvutsavam (annual festival) from March 3–9, 2026**. Key lines, translated in full:

- The sanctum is described as standing "revered like the morning light over Kumbham," the Malayalam month in which the festival falls, granting boons to worshippers as the bestower of all auspiciousness — radiating the grace of the Grama Devata (village deity) **Sree Panayakkalamma**.
- After the golden **Thiruvabharanam** (sacred ornaments) are ceremonially placed on the **moolavigraham** (main idol), the fully-adorned Devi grants sacred darshan for all seven days of the festival.
- On the second day, **Uthram Pongala** is offered, "welcoming the stars" of devotees seeking spiritual fulfillment (a poetic reference to the pongala ritual being dedicated under devotees' own birth stars).
- Under the joint leadership of the named Tantri and Melshanti, the festival features **Kalamezhuthu, Pongala, Ezhunnallipp (processions), cultural programs, Mudiyettu, and Thookkam (hanging rituals)** — culminating in the sacred darshan of **Vadakkupurathu Guruthi**, after which the **Thirasheela** (ceremonial curtain) falls, marking the formal close of the festival.
- During the festival, devotees are told it is auspicious to also seek darshan of the sub-deities who "reside here in vibrant spiritual presence alongside the boon-giving Devi": **Sri Ganapathi, Sri Ayyappan, Durga Devi, Yakshiyamma, Brahmarakshas, and the Serpent deities.**
- The letter closes calling the festival days "golden opportunities... to offer all misdeeds and sins at the lotus feet of the Devi and attain liberation (moksha)," and cordially invites the recipient, along with relatives and friends, to "cooperate in temple matters, observe temple customs, and make the festival more successful."
- Signed on behalf of the Devaswom Managing Committee by C.P. Krishnan Nair (President) and Manoj Babu, Thoppilparambu (Secretary), dated Kattikkunnu, 10.02.2026.

### Translated description of the Devi

The Devi is described as verily the Mother of the Universe (Jagat Matha) — the **Parashakti** from whom all creation springs, the deity of creation, sustenance, and destruction. Though she assumed a fierce (**Rudra**) form to destroy the asura Darika, in her peaceful form she is **Lalithambika**, granting all wishes to sincere devotees; sincere prayer yields anything, while defiance brings total ruin. Iconographically she is described as:

- Dark as a monsoon cloud (kaarmukil, മേഘവർണ്ണ)
- Three-eyed (trinetra)
- Seated upon the neck/back of her vahana **Vetalam** (a demonic spirit-mount)
- Bearing a sword, a shield, a severed head, and Darika's own severed head in her many arms
- Garlanded with skulls (mundamala)
- Surrounded by **Bhootas, Pretas, Pisachas, and Mathrukas** as her retinue
- Described as the destroyer of terrible calamities, subduer of "the wicked Asuri"

This all-boon-giving, Shakti-incarnate Jagat Matha is said to reside at the "ancient sacred Sree Panayakkal temple, which has become a holy destination" for pilgrims.

### Meaning of each Deeparadhana, as explained in the booklet

- **Alankara Deeparadhana** — performed right after abhishekam, once the Devi is adorned and offered sweet delicacies; devotees are told its darshan dispels karmic defects carried over from past lives.
- **Uchapooja Deeparadhana** — its darshan is said to remove all sins and grant prosperity.
- **Sandhya Deeparadhana** — performed at dusk; grants all-round prosperity.
- **Athazhapooja Deeparadhana** — the night pooja's lamp offering; its darshan is said to grant marital happiness.

### Other recurring festival-week observances

- **Nadaykkal Para** (measure-filling ritual at the temple entrance): 8:00–11:00 AM and 5:00–8:00 PM on festival days.
- **Pakal Pooram** (daytime procession): evenings of the 3rd–4th festival days.
- **Devaswom Thalappoli**: March 9.
- **Desa Thalam Varavu** (the desam's ceremonial thalam arrives): evenings of the 5th, 6th, and 7th festival days, from 7 PM.
- **Padapuja**: 7th festival day morning.
- **Darikan & Garudan Thookkam, Mudiyettu**: 7th festival day evening.
- **Vadakkupurathu Guruthi**: pre-dawn, 4:30 AM, closing the festival.

## 6. Major Rituals — Full Legends & Meaning

### Kalamezhuthu Pattu (all 7 festival evenings, 7:30 PM)
Devotees draw the divine form of Sree Bhagavathi on the temple-courtyard mandapam and sing of her deeds (apadanas), beginning right after Athazhapooja each night.

**Legend:** After slaying Darika, Devi's wrath would not subside; Ganapathi and Subrahmanya appeared before her disguised as orphaned children to console her, and her anger briefly cooled seeing them. But when she then saw her own image drawn on the rock that is the seat of her vahana Nandikeshan, her fury returned, and she declared: *"Whoever draws my form and prays shall receive my grace and prosperity."* Separately, when Narada Maharishi showed Lord Shiva the Devi's fearsome form — readied for Darika-vadham, with 64 arms and 64 weapons — Shiva blessed it, saying those who draw and worship the Kali form, and those who sing her praises, would be cured of disease and granted all prosperity. White, yellow, red, and black are the traditional colours used in the kalam (floor-drawing). Once the accompanying song concludes, the drawing is ceremonially erased with flowers, and the leftover kalam-powder is distributed to devotees as prasadam.

**Vazhipadu rate:** 1 day — ₹200; 7 days — ₹1,000.

### Uthram Pongala (main festival Pongala day, March 4)
A communal rice-offering ritual: devotees observing 7 days of vratham (fasting/austerity) light fires under hundreds of pongala pots simultaneously; as the fire rises and the rice boils over, sins and sorrows are said to dissolve into the atmosphere along with the rising steam/smoke. The booklet gives this symbolism: rice represents the universe, the husk/outer grain represents "Aham" (ego/I-ness) that dissolves as the rice cooks, jaggery represents bliss, and coconut represents the body. It is described as the highest of the five great sacrifices (Pancha Mahayajnas) — an "Anna Pooja" (food-worship) that is fundamentally Amma Daivam (Mother Goddess) worship.

**Pongala materials:** available at the Devaswom counter for ₹500; Pongala offering fee ₹50 (₹100 if devotees bring their own materials from outside instead of buying at the counter).

### Darikan Thookkam (7th day, 8:00 PM)
Depicts the arrogant asura Darika — made overconfident by a boon from Brahma that only a woman not born of a womb could kill him — challenging every direction to battle. The scene is enhanced with Chenda melam (drum ensemble), Thellikkathikkal, and Thalappoli. Relatives and friends of the performer are hosted at the performer's home; the performer dresses in costume, makeup, and crown there, does a home pradakshinam (circumambulation), and then walks to the temple alongside the percussion troupe.

### Garudan Thookkam (7th day, 8:45 PM)
Devotees portray Garuda on traditional hanging/swinging apparatus decorated with lamps.

**Legend:** For Darika-nigraham (Darika's destruction), Kalidevi (the Devi) went to war per Shiva's instruction. Darika's wife Danavathi, having pleased Parvati through severe austerity, was granted vasoori (smallpox) seeds by Gauri; in battle, Danavathi hurled these at the Devi, afflicting her with pain. The Devi's vahana Vethalam drank all of Darika's blood but remained unsatiated; the Devi then promised the Vethalam the blood of whoever next entered the battlefield. Unable to locate Kalidevi (who had gone off to battle), Shiva sent Vishnu's vahana Garuda to search for her — but the Devi could not permit Garuda's blood to be taken given her word to the Vethalam. Understanding this through his own wisdom, Garuda voluntarily offered his own blood; a single drop satisfied the Vethalam. This is given as the legendary origin of striking the gong (Chunda) at the end of Garudan Thookkam, marking the symbolic "spilling" of blood.

### Mudiyettu (7th day, 11:00 PM onward)
An extremely rare vazhipadu performed only at Bhagavathi shrines (Kavu). "Mudiyettu" literally means "taking on" Darika's severed head.

**Legend:** When the 14 worlds suffered under the asura emperors Darika and Danavendra — made arrogant by Brahma's boon that no man could kill them — they sought refuge with Shiva; Narada informed Shiva of their misdeeds; Shiva created Sree Bhadrakali from his third eye, who wages fierce war and destroys them, concluding with Mudiyettu. Performed as a vazhipadu against smallpox, skin ailments, ill omens, and for general wish-fulfillment and removal of adversity.

### Vadakkupurathu Guruthi (Great Guruthi, pre-dawn, close of the festival)
A square platform is erected outside the northern Gopuram using banana stems, areca-palm flowers, banana clusters, and jaggery; kaithiri (coconut-frond) and stick-torches are lit. After a procession accompanied by drums (kottu, kurava), the Devi is seated on the peedom (ceremonial pedestal), and Melshanti Brahmasri Appukuttan Namboothiri leads the Maha Guruthi. Rice powder, flower powder, and turmeric powder are mixed to make one vessel of "**Ninam**" and six vessels of "**Guruthi**" (a traditional turmeric-lime substitute for blood), poured onto the platform.

**Meaning:** After Darika-vadham, the still blood-thirsty Devi is offered this blood-like Guruthi to appease her. The booklet notes that in earlier times, actual animal-blood offerings (Tharppanam) were made at this point in the ritual's history. It concludes with Guruthi prasada distribution and the Devi's return to the Sreelakam (inner sanctum) for 7 days of closed nada, before the 7th-day (Ezhampooja) darshan.

**Vazhipadu rates:** Ninam — ₹200; Guruthi — ₹100; Sampoorna (Complete) Guruthi — ₹1,000.

### Maha Prasada Oottu (annadanam / feast, held on all Punartham & Utsavam days)
A large-scale food-charity ritual held at the sanctum of Bhagavathi and Sri Ayyappa Swami (lord of annadanam). The text frames "Anna" (food) as energy/life-force, so both the giver and receiver of food gain from the act — describing annadanam as spiritually superior even to grand material donations (gifting a thousand tusked elephants, a crore cows, gem-studded gold, land, or an ocean's worth of property).

**Rates:** Utsava Prasada Oottu — ₹17,000; Punartham Prasada Oottu — ₹15,000; 2026 Utsava contribution (a smaller shared-cost option) — ₹700.

### Ezhampooja (7th/closing Pooja) — March 15, 2026 (Meenam 1, Thiruvonam) Sunday
The day the Devi, who has been in "peaceful slumber" for 7 days following the Guruthi, grants darshan again to devotees — described as extra-auspicious in 2026 for coinciding with both Ekadasi and Meena Sankramam. Includes a special 10:00 AM rite: **Akamkalathil Ezhaam Guruthi Tharppanam** (the 7th Guruthi offering, performed within the inner enclosure). 12:30 PM: Ezhampooja Prasada Oottu — sponsored (2026) by Sreepathmanabhan M.K., Madathil, Kattikkunnu.

## 7. Other Annual Observances

### Vishu Kani — April 15, 2026 (Medam 1), Wednesday
On Vishu morning, Sree Krishna Bhagavan's Vishukani (the auspicious first-sight arrangement) is set up in the golden mandapam at the Devi's sanctum. Devotees may offer Vishu Kanikka.

### Pathamudayam (10th Sunrise) — April 23, 2026 (Medam 10, Punartham), Thursday
Considered the day the Sun's influence — strongest of the nine planets — peaks, making it highly auspicious for any undertaking. Devotees may observe a full 10-day vratham from Vishu day through Pathamudayam, or simply observe Pathamudayam alone: brahmacharya and avoidance of tamasic food are emphasized, concluding with temple darshan and theertham.

### Vinayaka Chaturthi — September 14, 2026, Monday
6:30 AM Mahaganapathi Homam plus special poojas/vazhipadu for Sree Ganapathi Bhagavan, the sub-deity at the temple. Invocation verse printed: *"Adipoojyam Ganadhyaksham, Gauriputram Vinayakam, Mangalam Paramam Roopam, Sreeganesham Namamyaham!"*

**Rates:** Ashtadravya Ganapathi Homam — ₹301; Ganapathi Homam — ₹51 + 1 coconut.

### Ayilyam Pooja — November 2, 2026 (Thulam 16, Mannarasala Ayilyam day), Monday
Special pooja for the temple's serpent sub-deities, Nagarajavu and Nagayakshi.

**Main vazhipadus:** Sarpapooja, Noorupalum, Thalichukodala, Ayilyampooja.

### Navaratri — October 18–21, 2026 (Kanni 1–4)
Celebrates the Devi's nine forms (Kumari, Trimurthy, Kalyani, Rohini, Kalika, Chandika, Saraswathi, Shambhavi, Durga) alongside the temple's sub-deities.

- **Oct 18 (Sun):** Poojavaypu (book & weapon worship).
- **Oct 19 (Mon):** Durgashtami.
- **Oct 20 (Tue):** Mahanavami.
- **Oct 21 (Wed):** Vijayadashami with Poojayeduppu — 7:00 AM Saraswathi Pooja, 8:30 AM Pooja eduppu, 8:50 AM **Vidyarambham** (children's Harisree/first-letters ceremony), performed by Brahmasri Kadambanattillam Appukuttan Namboothiri.

### Mandalapooja — November 17, 2026 (Vrischikam 1) to December 27, 2026 (41 days)
41-day vratham period honouring Sree Dharmasastha (Ayyappan), the sub-deity enshrined at the temple; nada opens daily for pooja/vazhipadu, with Punartham Oottu held throughout the period.

**Main pooja:** Mandala Chirappu Pooja — ₹200.

### Deepavali — November 8, 2026, Sunday
Commemorates Sree Krishna's slaying of Narakasura and Sri Rama's return after 14 years in exile. Pooja, vazhipadu, and decorative lights are held at the temple.

### Dhanumasa Thiruvathira — December 24, 2026, Thursday
Shiva's birthday and the wedding anniversary of Shiva and Parvati; an important day for women, some of whom observe vratham from Makayiram star. Gopis are said to have worshipped Karthyayani on this day to obtain Balagopalan (Krishna) as husband; new couples observe "Poothiruvathira." Pooja and vazhipadu are held at the temple.

## 8. Sub-Deities (Upadevas) at the Temple

| Deity | Description (translated) |
|---|---|
| **Durga Devi** | One of five goddesses (with Saraswathi, Savithri, Radha, Lakshmi) who emerged to enable creation; grants intellect, sleep, nourishment, fame, liberation, and happiness. Described as supreme and truthful. |
| **Yakshiyamma** | The consciousness-form of Parashakti as protector of karma; a Desa Devata (regional deity) believed to fulfil all requests of those who seek refuge. Astrologically significant — often the first deity to manifest when a Prashnam (astrological consultation) is done regarding this temple, which is why devotees from distant places also come to worship her here. |
| **Brahmarakshas** | The protective Brahma-tejas (divine energy), worshipped in the concept of a consciousness-endowed Vishnu; considered the owner of the temple land and holder of the Guru position. |
| **Nagarajavu (Vasuki) & Nagayakshi** | Serpent deities worshipped as family/clan deities for progeny, wealth and prosperity — angering them is said to bring misfortune. Best offerings: Thalichu Kodala, Paal Payasam (milk payasam). |
| **Sri Ganapathi** | Removes obstacles; worshipped first before any undertaking, just as even the Devas worshipped him before Palazhi Madhanam (the churning of the ocean of milk). |
| **Sri Dharmasastha (Ayyappan)** | A fierce, luminous form who metes punishment/blessing in Kali Yuga; honoured especially during the 41-day Mandalapooja. |

## 9. Darshan Protocol (Temple Visiting Order)

Translated from the booklet's "ദർശനക്രമം" section:

1. Outside the Chuttambalam (outer temple courtyard), first bow and pray to Yakshiyamma, Brahmarakshas, Nagarajavu, and Nagayakshi.
2. Enter through the **eastern entrance**, praying to the Devi as you enter the Chuttambalam.
3. Turn **left** and walk to pray to Sri Ganapathi, Dharmasastha, and Durga Devi; bow, then proceed to the front of the Sreekovil to pray.
4. Turn left again, receive theertham (holy water) and prasadam from the Melshanti, and complete the pradakshinam (circumambulation).
5. Exit through the **northern entrance**.
6. **Entry through the southern and western entrances is prohibited.**
7. Devotees are asked to observe mounam (silence) and carry the memory of the darshan with them.
